## Market size and buyer

### Takeaway
The addressable lives are about 35.2M in Medicare Advantage and over 66M in Medicaid MCOs, and both markets are concentrated in a few parents. I found no verified benchmark for care management PMPM spend or staff per 1,000 members, and published ROI is mixed.

### Cited Findings

**Enrollment**
- Medicare Advantage 2026: 35.2 million of 64.2 million eligible beneficiaries, 55% ([KFF, 5 Jun 2026, updated 1 Jul 2026](https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-in-2026-enrollment-update-and-key-trends/)).
- MA parent share 2026: UnitedHealth Group 26% (about 9.3M), Humana 20% (about 7M), CVS Health 12%, Kaiser 6%, Elevance 5% (same KFF page).
- Medicaid managed care, as of 1 Jul 2024: over 66 million in comprehensive MCOs, 78% of beneficiaries; 291 MCOs ([KFF, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/10-things-to-know-about-medicaid-managed-care/)).
- Medicaid concentration 2024: Centene, UnitedHealth, Elevance, Molina and Aetna/CVS hold 47% of MCO enrollment; 15 multi-state parents hold over 62%. MCO payments were about 50% of $919B FY2024 Medicaid spending. MLR rose from 88% in 2023 to 91% in 2024 (same KFF page).

**Buyer signals (weak; quoted executives, not proof of purchasing authority)**
- Sentara Health Plans picking Jiva: quote from SVP and Chief Medical Officer; more than 875,000 members ([PR Newswire, 2 Mar 2021](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/sentara-health-plans-selects-jiva-to-enable-rigorous-clinical-management-at-maximum-efficiency-301237924.html)).
- CareFirst picking GuidingCare: quotes from EVP Health Services (an RN) and EVP/CIO ([release, 22 Sep 2020](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/carefirst-bluecross-blueshield-selects-altruista-healths-care-management-workflow-platform-to-serve-3-4-million-members)).
- Plan executive intent: nearly 80% plan to buy or co-develop AI with vendors; 86% say they are not fully ready to operationalize AI at scale; n=63 insurers' C-suite leaders [VENDOR survey by Innovaccer] ([HIT Consultant, 14 May 2026](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/05/14/innovaccer-2026-payer-ai-report/)).

**Published ROI (mixed)**
- Positive, observational, MA: HIP geriatric case management, 101 participants vs 1,585 eligible nonparticipants. Costs $7,720 lower per participant, program cost $2,755 per managed member, net $4,965. Self-selection is a risk ([Picariello, Pop Health Manag 2008](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/impact-of-a-geriatric-case-management-program-on-health-plan-cost-2/)).
- Null, randomized, Medicare FFS: three demonstrations did not improve self-care or health or reduce Medicare spending. Cost $130-$373 per client-month in 1990s dollars. One project increased admissions ([Schore, Health Care Financ Rev 1999](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194601)).
- Pharmacy care management, MA: 724 members vs 3,620 controls, net $108 per enrollee per month, $406 in the high-score subgroup. Study by UPMC Health Plan and RxAnte; this page is a press release from an RxAnte subsidiary and gives no CI [VENDOR] ([Mosaic Pharmacy, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.mosaicpharmacy.com/insights-and-resources/study-reveals-positive-outcomes)).
- Cigna's and other plans' current ROI: see Unverified.

**Staffing and caseload**
- Iowa Medicaid managed care, community-based case managers: average 45 members, maximum 50, from Jan 2025 ([Iowa HHS](https://hhs.iowa.gov/medicaid/about-medicaid/medicaid-projects/home-project/case-management)). This is a high-touch LTSS-type ratio, not a plan-wide one.
- No single benchmark exists: a CMSA/NASW paper found "average" caseloads from 2 to 365. A survey of case managers found 25-49 cases per week as the most common band (26.5%). Author is a TCS Healthcare Technologies executive ([CMSA Pulse, Mar 2014](https://www.naylornetwork.com/cmsapulse/articles/index-v2.asp?aid=258817&issueID=30163)).

### Inferences
- Sizing: 35.2M + 66M is about 101M lives, but this double-counts dual eligibles and mixes 2026 with 2024 data. Say "about 100M, with overlap."
- Medicaid enrollment has probably fallen since July 2024, per unverified HMA data below, so don't present 66M as current.
- Rising Medicaid MLR (91% in 2024) suggests admin budgets are tight, which favors a cost-out pitch over a platform replacement. That link is my inference.
- Likely buyer: a clinical executive (CMO, EVP Health Services, or VP Care Management) as sponsor, with the CIO as co-approver. The two press releases fit this pattern but do not establish it, and I found nothing naming "VP Clinical Ops." Validate with 3-5 interviews.
- ROI slide: do not promise medical cost savings. Evidence on care management itself is contested, so anchor the pitch on staff capacity and auditability, which the product can actually measure.

## Ambient AI scribes and LLM drafting evidence

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence shows real but modest time savings, roughly 16-22 minutes a day or tens of seconds per note, and no reliable reduction in after-hours work. Burnout results are more consistent. Errors are common enough that clinician review is required, and omissions are the dominant failure. All of this comes from clinicians in visits, not from payer case managers.

### Cited Findings

**Time savings and burnout**

| Study | Design | Population | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lukac, NEJM AI 2025 ([UCLA](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/ucla-study-finds-ai-scribes-may-reduce-documentation-time)) | 3-arm RCT, Nov 2024-Jan 2025, single academic center | 238 physicians, 14 specialties, 72,000 encounters | Nabla 9.5% greater time-in-note reduction than control (4:30 to 3:49, about 41 s, vs control about 18 s). DAX: not significant |
| Afshar, NEJM AI 2025 ([Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/ambient-ai-scribes-linked-lower-work-exhaustion-multistate-pragmatic-trial)) | Stepped-wedge RCT, 24 weeks, 2 states | 66 practitioners, 71,487 notes | Exhaustion -0.44 on a 5-point scale (CI -0.62 to -0.25). Notes -0.36 h/day (CI -0.55 to -0.17). Work outside work -0.50 h/day, not robust to removing outliers. Vendor not named on this page |
| Rotenstein, JAMA 2026 ([Mass General Brigham](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/ai-scribes-linked-to-modest-reductions-in-ehr-documentation-time)) | Multisite observational, 5 hospitals | 1,800+ users, 6,770 controls | About 16 min/day less documentation (10%), 13 min/day less EHR (3%), 0.5 more visits/week, $167/month revenue. After-hours EHR time unchanged. Only 32% of users used it in over 50% of visits |
| Olson, JAMA Netw Open 2025 ([JAMA Netw Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2839542)) | Pre-post, no control, Feb-Oct 2024, 6 systems | 263 clinicians (186 for burnout); Abridge; 60.3% completion | Burnout 51.9% to 38.8%. After-hours documentation 0.90 h less, self-reported. Two authors have Abridge ties |
| TPMG, NEJM Catalyst 2025 ([AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/ai-scribes-save-15000-hours-and-restore-human-side-medicine)) | 63-week observational, Oct 2023-Dec 2024 | 7,260 physicians, 2,576,627 encounters | Estimated 15,791 hours saved. 82% reported improved work satisfaction. No accuracy data reported |
| Tierney, NEJM Catalyst 2024 ([KP record](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/blog/publications/ambient-artificial-intelligence-scribes-to-alleviate-the-burden-of-clinical-documentation/)) | 10-week rollout | 3,442 physicians, up to 303,266 encounters; 968 used it in ≥100 | No numeric time, quality or error results |

- **Conflict:** Olson's self-reported 0.90 h after-hours reduction contrasts with Rotenstein's objective finding of no significant change. Prefer the EHR-log finding.
- **Unit caution:** press coverage says per day, while one search summary says normalized to 8 scheduled patient hours. Check the JAMA paper before writing "per day."
- Financial return: a March 2025 article on the Peterson Health Technology Institute report says the financial impact on health systems is "unclear," and higher-level coding may increase spending ([HC Innovation Group](https://www.hcinnovationgroup.com/analytics-ai/generative-ai/news/55277198/report-ambient-scribes-help-with-burnout-but-financial-impact-unclear)).

**Note quality and error rates**
- Lukac: physicians said notes "occasionally" had clinically significant inaccuracies, mostly omissions and pronoun errors; one mild safety event ([UCLA](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/ucla-study-finds-ai-scribes-may-reduce-documentation-time)).
- Afshar: PDSQI-9 quality domain means of 3.97-4.99 of 5 ([Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/ambient-ai-scribes-linked-lower-work-exhaustion-multistate-pragmatic-trial)).
- Anderson, Mayo Clin Proc Digit Health 2025, 5 platforms, 14 simulated encounters: 26.3% of key elements omitted or wrong (CI 17.0-31.0). Omissions were 76.3% of note errors. Mean 3.0 errors per case of moderate or higher harm potential (range 0-21). Medication errors were the most common type, and 5 of 13 severe errors were medication-related. Funded by NIH and AHRQ, no competing interests ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12605248/)). Simulated audio, so it may not match live performance. Do not quote the overall mean errors per case: the figure read (16.4) sits outside its stated CI (9.0-11.0).
- Asgari, npj Digit Med 2025, GPT-4-32k, mock primary care consultations: hallucinations 1.47% (191 of 12,999 note sentences), 44% of them major; omissions 3.45% (1,712 of 49,590 transcript sentences), 16.7% major. Pooled over 18 experimental setups; prompt iteration changed results ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/12075489/)). London-based authors using mock data, so this is outside the US-only scope and is included only as the most-quoted benchmark. Label it as non-US.

**LLM summarization of records**
- Williams, JAMA Intern Med 2025, UCSF, 100 inpatient encounters, 22 attending reviewers, blinded: LLM summaries averaged 2.91 errors vs 1.82 for physicians. Overall quality equivalent; no significant difference in potential harm; LLM more concise and coherent but less comprehensive ([The Hospitalist](https://www.the-hospitalist.org/hospitalist/article/40519/in-the-literature/llms-and-physicians-generate-comparable-hospital-discharge-summaries/)). Single center.

**Medication extraction**
- Shao 2025 preprint abstract, 12 LLMs: GPT-4o zero-shot average F1 of 94.0% for extraction, 78.1% for discontinuation classification, 72.7% for the joint task. Medical-specific LLMs scored lower than general ones ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11137v2)).

### Inferences
- Calculated from the TPMG figures: 15,791 hours over 2,576,627 encounters is about 22 seconds per encounter. This matches Lukac's net of about 23 seconds per note (41 s minus 18 s). "Hours saved" headlines hide a small per-note effect.
- Calculated from Shao: extracting a medication name is far easier than saying whether it is still current. The second task is the one that matters for a case manager, and it is where accuracy falls.
- Calculated from Anderson and Asgari: omission is the main failure, and a reviewer cannot spot what is absent from a draft without the source. The workbench should show source links and flag what it did not find.
- Transfer risk: all time evidence comes from visit documentation. A case manager's work is chart review, outreach and care plans, so savings could be larger or smaller. Measure this; don't borrow a number.
- Adoption matters as much as accuracy: only 32% were heavy users in Rotenstein, and they captured most of the gain.

### Gaps
- Unverified:
  - Lukac's CIs and DAX -1.7%: PubMed snippet only (PubMed refused the fetch). The vendor/funding status beyond the listed UCLA and NIH sources was not confirmed.
  - Rotenstein 8,581 total and exact 13.4/16.0 minutes: snippet only.
  - MedStar study (JMIR 2025) 2.9 errors per draft note, 31 of 44 notes: page returned no readable text.
  - UC Davis 94.7% of notes free of significant errors: snippet only.
  - Suki study 31% vs 20% hallucination rate: secondary snippet only.
  - JMIR Med Inform 2026 time-motion 15.0% reduction: page returned no readable text; country not confirmed.
  - arXiv 2604.21076 medication reconciliation F1 on synthetic patients: not opened.
  - PHTI report PDF: downloaded but no text extraction tool was available. Suki's $54,000 per user claim and vendor prices of $99-$299 per provider per month: snippets only.
- No study found of medication reconciliation accuracy by an LLM on real US EHR data, or of LLM drafting for payer case management.
- JAMA Network Open 2024-2026 RCTs: the Olson paper is not an RCT, and the RCTs I found are in NEJM AI. A JAMA Network Open RCT may exist that I did not surface.

## Payer-side deployments and the nH Predict caution

### Takeaway
Payers are spending heavily on member-facing and call-center AI, but I found no published, verifiable result on case manager time savings at a large payer. That is both a white space and a warning: nobody has public proof yet. nH Predict shows the risk when a model appears to replace clinical judgment.

### Cited Findings

**Deployments (trade aggregator, not primary; verify before quoting)** ([DistilINFO, 30 Apr 2026](https://distilinfo.com/2026/04/30/5-ways-insurers-are-betting-big-on-ai/)):
- UnitedHealth Group: $1.5B AI spending plan for 2026; Avery member assistant; 26 million calls directed by AI agents in 2025.
- Elevance: $1B AI commitment; virtual assistant reaching 22 million commercial members; Health OS used by 60,000+ associates.
- Humana: Agent Assist with Google Cloud, no figures.
- Cigna: member chatbot and provider matching, no figures.
- Optum Value Connect: 29% fewer ER visits and 28% fewer preventable readmissions, customer-reported [VENDOR].

**nH Predict**
- Estate of Lokken v. UnitedHealth, D. Minn., filed Nov 2023. On 13 Feb 2025 Judge Tunheim dismissed several claims as preempted but let breach of contract and implied covenant claims proceed. Plaintiffs allege "over 90% of claim denials are reversed on appeal" ([BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/court-unitedhealth-must-answer-for-ai-based-claim-denials-a-27534)).
- On 9 Mar 2026 the court granted a motion to compel broad discovery, including nH Predict documents and AI review board materials. Optum says the tool is care-support and physicians make medical necessity decisions ([ArentFox Schiff, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/federal-court-orders-broad-discovery-against-uhc-ai-coverage-denial-lawsuit)). No merits ruling found; these remain allegations.
- Senate PSI report (Oct 2024), via provider trade group LeadingAge: UHC SNF initial denials rose from 1.4% in 2019 to 12.6% in 2022. Humana denied 24.6% of post-acute requests, 16x its overall rate. CVS denied 25.9%. The report says most tools tested generated more denials ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/analysis-senate-report-on-ma-plans-reveals-troubling-data/)). LeadingAge represents post-acute providers, so it has a stake.
- CMS's 6 Feb 2024 FAQ allows AI in MA coverage determinations, but decisions must reflect the individual patient's circumstances and predictive outputs alone are not sufficient. CMS flagged criteria drift and bias ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/cms-confirms-medicare-advantage-organizations-may-use-ai-in-making-coverage/)).
- Buyer sentiment: 75% said AI workflows need human review, explainability, monitoring and audit trails before broad deployment; n=8,194 payer technology users ([Black Book, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.newswire.com/news/black-book-polls-find-health-plans-moving-from-digital-transformation-22789286)).

### Inferences
- Design line for the deck: AI drafts and surfaces, and a licensed clinician decides. The workbench should never produce a coverage recommendation, and should stay out of utilization management in the first release. This is my recommendation, not legal advice; have counsel review.
- Position human review and audit trails as a purchase requirement rather than a limitation, since Black Book's 75% suggests buyers already expect them.

### Gaps
- Unverified:
  - Cigna AI care coordination and "$2,000 per year" savings for high-risk patients: BenefitsPro and Quartz blocked, snippets only.
  - UHC post-acute denials 10.9% to 22.7% (2020-2022): the primary Senate PDF and Blumenthal release blocked. Note this differs in scope from LeadingAge's SNF figures, so never mix them.
  - "Prior authorizations cut by nearly 70%": a search summary attributed it to UnitedHealth, while the DistilINFO page read attributes it to Elevance. Do not use it until checked.
  - Regional plan "72% documentation time reduction": unnamed, from a vendor blog. Do not use.
- No primary source found for AI use by nurse case managers at Humana, Elevance, UnitedHealth or Cigna.

## Alert fatigue

### Takeaway
High override rates are well documented, but they are drug-alert order-entry data from clinical settings and do not prove clinicians were wrong: many overrides were appropriate. The lesson for a workbench is to send fewer, better-targeted prompts. Don't assume a nudge will be acted on.

### Cited Findings
- 49-96% of drug safety alerts overridden; review with data from studies published 2000-2004, mostly US teaching hospitals. Serious overdose alerts about a quarter. Overriding "may often be justified." Adverse drug events did not occur in over 97% of overrides in chart review studies ([van der Sijs, JAMIA 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1447540)). The paper says 17 publications but only nine had quantitative override data.
- Outpatients: 53% of alerts overridden, and half of those should have been addressed ([Nanji 2014, via AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/overrides-medication-related-clinical-decision-support-alerts-outpatients)).
- Drug allergy alerts, over 150,000: 81% overridden inpatient, 77% outpatient, over 96% of overrides judged appropriate ([Slight 2016, via PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/cross-sectional-observational-study-high-override-rates-drug-allergy-alerts-inpatient-and)).
- AHRQ: clinicians "override the vast majority" of CPOE warnings. A VA primary care study reported over 100 alerts per day. Recommended fixes: higher specificity, patient tailoring, severity tiers, and interruptive alerts only for high severity ([PSNet primer, updated 15 Dec 2024](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/alert-fatigue)).
- Predictive model example: Epic Sepsis Model at Michigan, 38,455 hospitalizations: AUC 0.63, sensitivity 33%, PPV 12%, alerting on 18% of patients ([JAMA Intern Med editorial, 2021](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781313)).

### Inferences
- Calculated from PPV 12%: about 8 alerts reviewed per true case. Vendors should publish PPV and alert burden, not only AUC.
- Slide wording: "In the clinical literature, 49-96% of drug-safety alerts were overridden (2006 review), and many of those overrides were appropriate." Avoid "clinicians ignore 96% of alerts."
- Pilot metrics should include alerts per case manager per day, acceptance rate, and a sample audit of dismissed alerts.

### Gaps
- No override data found for payer care management workflows; applying this evidence to them is an extrapolation.
- Ancker 2017 (workload and repeated alerts): the PSNet page holds only the citation, so no findings are asserted.

## Pricing signals and pilot design

### Takeaway
There is no public PMPM price for payer care management software. Only seat prices are public, and PMPM anchors have to be built and labeled as estimates. No source sets a norm for payer AI pilots, so the design below is a proposal supported by how comparable studies ran.

### Cited Findings

**Price signals**
- Salesforce Health Cloud: $350-$725 per user per month list ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/healthcare-life-sciences/health-cloud/pricing/)).
- Welkin Health: care management software is often per license or per user per month; Welkin $399/month per license plus an implementation fee, with no PMPM data. Provider-oriented, dated Jan 2024 [VENDOR] ([Welkin](https://welkinhealth.com/care-management-software-cost/)).
- HiLabs: estimated ContractsAI savings of $2.47 PMPY; $20M annual provider-data cost at a 4M-member plan [VENDOR] ([hilabs.com](https://www.hilabs.com/)).
- Buyers increasingly expect unit-cost transparency and cost-per-transaction measures, and want evidence of gains in cycle time, first-pass accuracy and defect rates; "no longer impressed by disconnected automation pilots" ([Black Book, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.newswire.com/news/black-book-polls-find-health-plans-moving-from-digital-transformation-22789286)). No pricing data in that release.
- AI budget intent: about 75% of payers intend to spend over $10M on AI-driven payment and care outcome initiatives over 3-5 years; nearly a third of national plans have earmarked $50M+. n=63 [VENDOR survey] ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/05/14/innovaccer-2026-payer-ai-report/)).

**Durations seen in comparable evaluations (not payer SaaS)**
- 30 days ([Olson](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2839542)); about 2 months ([Lukac](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/ucla-study-finds-ai-scribes-may-reduce-documentation-time)); 10 weeks ([Tierney](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/blog/publications/ambient-artificial-intelligence-scribes-to-alleviate-the-burden-of-clinical-documentation/)); 24 weeks stepped-wedge ([Afshar](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/ambient-ai-scribes-linked-lower-work-exhaustion-multistate-pragmatic-trial)).
- Innovaccer's "within 90 days" claim shows the time window vendors sell against [VENDOR] ([release](https://www.newswire.com/news/innovaccer-ranks-1-in-ai-agent-based-health-plan-technology-22591699)).

### Inferences
All arithmetic below is mine, and each is an order-of-magnitude anchor, not a benchmark.
- HiLabs $2.47 PMPY is about $0.21 PMPM of claimed savings; its $20M at 4M members is about $0.42 PMPM of claimed cost.
- Seat price conversion: a hypothetical 1M-member plan with an assumed 100 care managers at $350/seat pays $35,000/month, about $0.035 PMPM. The 100 is an assumption, not a benchmark.
- Program cost for comparison: Picariello's $2,755 per managed member per year is about $230 per managed member per month, so software is a small slice next to staff cost.
- Slide suggestion: "Pricing signal: public list prices are per seat ($350-$725 per user per month, Salesforce). Payer contract PMPMs are not published; test willingness to pay directly."
- Proposed pilot, to be validated with buyers: 90 days at one plan and line of business; 20-50 care managers; a stepped or concurrent control rather than pre-post only, because Lukac's control arm also improved (18 s); 2-4 week baseline before launch.
  - Primary metric: EHR-log-style time per case, not self-report.
  - Safety metrics: audited omission and error rate per draft, especially medications; share of drafts edited.
  - Adoption: share of eligible cases using AI, since Rotenstein showed gains concentrate in heavy users.
  - Alert burden: prompts per user per day and acceptance rate.
  - Stop rule: agreed in advance, tied to major error rate.

### Gaps
- Unverified:
  - Generic chronic care software $0.99-$8 per patient per month (HealthArc, 2022 estimate): snippet only, provider-side.
  - CPC+ average care management fee of about $15 (Track 1) and $28 (Track 2): CMS PDF unreadable, and the CMS page gives no amounts. It is a provider payment, not software, though it would be a useful proxy.
  - Louisiana $1.50 PCP care management fee: snippet only, provider payment.
  - Wavect 30-day AI pilot scorecard: snippet only, general and not payer-specific.
- No public contract value found for Jiva, GuidingCare or TruCare. State procurement portals or public records requests might help, particularly for public plans.
- No published payer SaaS pilot length, success-rate or conversion norm found. KLAS Points of Light 2026 was not opened.

## What AI can automate versus what a clinician must confirm

### Takeaway
The evidence supports AI drafting and retrieval with clinician sign-off. It does not support unreviewed clinical statements, medication status, or anything resembling a coverage decision.

### Cited Findings
- Omissions are the dominant scribe error, at 76.3% ([Anderson 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12605248/)).
- Medication errors are the most common error type on simulated encounters (same source).
- LLM summaries contain more errors than physician ones (2.91 vs 1.82), with similar harm potential and overall quality ([Williams 2025](https://www.the-hospitalist.org/hospitalist/article/40519/in-the-literature/llms-and-physicians-generate-comparable-hospital-discharge-summaries/)).
- Medication extraction F1 is 94.0%, falling to 72.7% when status is included (preprint) ([Shao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11137v2)).
- Decisions must reflect individual circumstances, and predictive output alone is insufficient for MA coverage ([Reed Smith on CMS FAQ](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/cms-confirms-medicare-advantage-organizations-may-use-ai-in-making-coverage/)).
- 75% of payer tech users want human review and audit trails before broad deployment ([Black Book 2026](https://www.newswire.com/news/black-book-polls-find-health-plans-moving-from-digital-transformation-22789286)).

### Inferences
The tiers are my judgment from the evidence above, with no source publishing this framework:

| Tier | Tasks | Basis and honest statement |
|---|---|---|
| AI can do, spot-check | Ingest and normalize records; find documents; pre-fill demographics; timeline of encounters; draft outreach logistics | Low clinical judgment. "Standardization accuracy" claims are vendor-reported, so measure on the plan's data |
| AI drafts, clinician edits and signs | Chart summary; case notes; call summary; suggested care plan goals | "AI writes a first draft. A clinician reviews every draft before it enters the record." Expect omissions; show source links |
| Clinician must confirm each item | Active medication list and changes; allergies; diagnoses new to the record; risk statements; social needs; member-stated preferences | Medication status is the weak spot (F1 72.7% joint, preprint) and carries severe-error risk |
| Clinician only, no AI recommendation | Medical necessity and coverage; program discharge; escalation or safety judgment; suicide risk or abuse assessment | CMS FAQ and the nH Predict litigation. Keep out of the first release |

Sentences that are safe to say:
- "Trials show modest time savings of about 10% of documentation time. We will measure yours rather than promise a number."
- "In studies, AI drafts mainly fail by leaving things out, so we show sources and require sign-off."

Sentences to avoid:
- "AI eliminates documentation."
- "Hallucination rate is 1.47%." That is one pooled, non-US, mock-consultation result on GPT-4.
- "Saves 15,000 hours." It is about 22 seconds per encounter, and is an estimate.
- Any claim that scribe evidence applies directly to case managers.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on AI drafting accuracy for payer care plans, assessments or case notes. A pilot would produce some of the first public data.
- Regulatory view covers MA coverage determinations; I did not research state AI laws or Medicaid-specific rules, so have counsel check them.
