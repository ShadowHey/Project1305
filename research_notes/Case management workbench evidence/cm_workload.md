# Workload, time use and working conditions of US payer RN case managers

Researched 6 Nov 2026. Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[PAYER]** = measured or stated for health-plan staff.
- **[PAYER-ADJACENT]** = Medicaid managed care rule or rate model; staff may sit at the MCO or a delegated entity, and may not all be RNs.
- **[PROXY-HOSPITAL]** or **[PROXY-OTHER]** = not payer data.
- **[VENDOR]** = vendor-authored or vendor-commissioned.

Method note: where a source was a PDF, text was extracted locally from the downloaded file (the Iowa, NSI, ACHP, NC DHHS, Blue Shield Promise and Ohio PDFs). Anything seen only in a search snippet, or on a page that blocked retrieval, is listed under "Unverified" and is not asserted.

## 1. What do published sources report on caseloads?

### Takeaway
No national standard exists, and I found no CMSA, CCMC, NCQA or URAC number that could be read and cited. The brief's complex CM range (30-60) and telephonic range (100-200+) both hold up, but the best payer evidence is from 2011 and 2022. The transitions range (60-100+) has no payer source and mixes concurrent caseload with monthly discharge flow, so it should be restated before use.

### Verdict on the brief's figures
| Brief figure | Verdict | Best support | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex CM 30-60 active cases per nurse | **Supported**; add that the highest-acuity programs run lower (10-29) | ACHP 2011 plan table (35-40, 40-60, ~50, 60); Iowa 45 avg / 50 max; NC 29:1 high and 46:1 medium; CA ECM 20-50 | Medium: only the ACHP table is RN or plan-employed care manager data, and it is 15 years old |
| Transitions of care 60-100+ | **Not validated; restate as stock vs flow** | No payer source. CTI coach 24-28 concurrent / ~300 per year (AHRQ; not payer, not call-center). The only higher numbers are second-hand in a forum post | Low |
| Telephonic DM 100-200+ | **Supported as a range** | ACHP 2011 (130-150; ~200 Medicaid PMAP; 80-200 work list); Virginia MCO median 144; NC low tier 87:1 to 142:1 | Medium: the Virginia and NC figures are not telephonic DM specifically |

### Cited Findings

**Payer or plan-reported**
- [PAYER] Care manager caseloads reported by 12 Alliance of Community Health Plans members in a side-by-side plan table (pp. 52-53): "10-15 complex case management patients per CM"; "Target is 60 per case manager"; "1.5 FTE manage 150 participants"; "130-150 per CM"; "50 per CM along with other duties such as post hospital phone calls"; "CM caseload of ~50 complex CM cases"; "35-40 patients per CM"; "40-60 patients per CM"; UCare by product: MSHO 75-100, UCare Connect 50-75, UCare for Seniors 50-80, PMAP ~200; "Most PBCMs have between 80-200 members on their work list". ACHP handbook, November 2011 — [ACHP](https://achp.org/wp-content/uploads/ACHP-Care-Management-Handbook.pdf). Caveat: PDF extraction loses the column alignment, so only the UCare values can safely be tied to a named plan. The cohort is non-profit community plans, not national carriers.
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Virginia Medicaid: median caseload of **144 members** among care coordinators at the state's 6 MCOs; almost two-thirds reported a caseload increase over the prior year. 322 surveys, 24% response of 1,318 coordinators; data April-July 2022, published 2025. Caseload and coordination time skew to LTSS members. The study is about SUD care coordination and the coordinators are not stated to be all RNs — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/challenges-faced-by-medicaid-managed-care-coordinators-working-with-members-with-substance-use-disorder).

**State Medicaid rules and contracts**
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Iowa: by January 1, 2025, community-based case manager (CBCM) to member ratios may not exceed a **statewide average of 45**, and **no single CBCM may exceed 50 members**. Applies to CBCMs serving people through HCBS under MCO contracts (Iowa Total Care, Molina, Wellpoint). Iowa HHS slides, November 21, 2024 — [Iowa HHS slides](https://hhs.iowa.gov/media/15084/download?inline=); restated at [Iowa HHS page](https://hhs.iowa.gov/medicaid/about-medicaid/medicaid-projects/home-project/case-management). The stated reason: case managers said large caseloads prevented relationships and limited responsiveness and service plan oversight.
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] North Carolina Tailored Plans: caseload assumptions per FTE care manager, actively engaged members, **with extenders absorbing part of the work**: High 29:1 (BH and I/DD/TBI); Medium 46:1 (both); Low 87:1 (BH), 142:1 (I/DD/TBI). NC DHHS states these were built for rate setting only and are not program requirements. One supervising care manager may oversee no more than 8 care managers (this one is a requirement). NC DHHS, January 19, 2022 — [NC DHHS](https://medicaid.ncdhhs.gov/updated-tailored-care-management-rate-guidance-jan-24-2022/download).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Ohio deliberately sets no number in the contract. Each MCO must implement maximum caseload sizes and may not exceed the sizes ODM pre-approves in its care coordination program submission. RNs are one of several accepted licenses for MCO Care Managers. Provider Agreement, Rev. 7/2026, Appendix D — [Ohio Medicaid](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/medicaid.ohio.gov/Providers/ProviderTypes/Managed%20Care/Provider%20Agreements/2026_07_MCO_Final_2.pdf).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] California ECM, Santa Cruz County: typical 20-50 members per care manager; 30-40 with support such as a CHW; 40-50 in team models; one organization 20-25 based on acuity. Undated FAQ, site copyright 2026, Central California Alliance for Health — [Alliance FAQ](https://thealliance.health/for-providers/manage-care/enhanced-care-management-and-community-supports-provider-faqs/).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Blue Shield of California Promise (Medi-Cal) reviews complex case management acuity monthly to determine resources for "appropriate staffing ratios" but states no number. Policy PHMCM-002, revised 4/2026 — [Blue Shield Promise](https://www.blueshieldca.com/content/dam/bsca/en/promise/clinical-policies-procedures/PHMCM-002-Complex-case-management.pdf).

**Professional bodies**
- [PROXY-OTHER, all settings] The CMSA/NASW Caseload Concept Paper (fall 2008) found "average caseloads" ranging from **2 to 365**, as reported by a [VENDOR] author, Pat Stricker of TCS Healthcare Technologies, undated, circa 2012-13 by its references — [CMSA Pulse](https://naylornetwork.com/cmsapulse/articles/print.asp?aid=258817). The same article reports a CMSA/TCS/ABQAURP survey of weekly caseloads: 25-49 cases 26.5%; 10-24 19.5%; 50-74 15%; over 100 11.5%; no routine caseload 16%. Those shares cover all settings, and the published [TCS slide version](https://www.slideshare.net/TCSHealthcare/what-is-an-average-caseload) shows chart percentages that do not match its text (37/27/21/16), so treat them as low quality.

**Transitions of care**
- [PROXY-OTHER] Care Transitions Intervention coach: about **24-28 recently discharged patients at a time, ~300 per year** (conservative estimate); caseload is driven mainly by how far apart patients live. AHRQ PSNet, last updated January 11, 2021. This is a home-visit model and is not payer-run — [AHRQ PSNet](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/innovation/transition-coachesr-reduce-readmissions-medicare-patients-complex-postdischarge-needs).
- [PROXY-OTHER, anecdotal] A nurse leader at Bronson Healthcare (Michigan) reported a TOC target of at least 12 calls a day on average, or about 240 patients per nurse per month if all discharges were followed. The same post relays Sg2 2016 (RN panels 30-150 for health coaches, 80-120 for contact center) and Advisory Board 2014 (non-RN coordinators 300-400 discharges per month). Forum thread, September 2024; I did not see the underlying reports — [AAACN forum](https://community.aaacn.org/discussion/caseloads-for-transitional-care-nurses).

### Inferences
- **A capacity cross-check on the brief's ranges, using NC's assumptions (my calculation, not a published result).** Assume 40 h/week, less 5 h non-member time, at 95% capacity: about 33 h/week, or about 143 h/month. Use 1 h per telephonic contact, plus 15-45 min per member per month for care team work.
  - DM-like, 1 contact/month + 15 min: 1.25 h per member, about **114 members**. Inside the brief's 100-200.
  - Complex, a contact every 14 days + 45 min: about 2.9 h per member, about **49 members**. Inside the brief's 30-60.
  - Very high-touch, 4 contacts/month + 45 min: 4.75 h per member, about **30 members**. Matches NC's 29:1.
  - Implication: the bottom of the brief's 100-200 range is what 1 h per contact allows. Reaching 200 requires shorter contacts or less frequent ones.
- **Transitions should be expressed in two units.** 60-100 *concurrent* members is roughly 2-4x the only verified model (24-28). 60-100 *discharges per month* is arithmetically consistent with call-based work: 143 h/month supports about 71 discharges at two 1-hour contacts each. At 12 calls a day, a call averages about 40 minutes all-in.
- Caseload is a weak metric across plans because of denominator differences: assigned vs actively engaged (NC counts engaged only), work list vs open case (the "PBCM" entry counts work list), and whether extenders are assumed (NC is).
- Contract ceilings near 45-50 sit in HCBS/LTSS programs with in-person work, so they are not direct evidence for telephonic RN complex CM.

### Gaps
- **No payer-specific caseload benchmark newer than 2011 for RN staff at commercial or national carriers was found.** The Virginia figure (2022) is the newest payer-employed number.
- **No payer TOC caseload source exists in anything I could open.**
- The CMSA Standards of Practice, the Case Load Capacity Calculator and the NCQA/URAC standards are paid or were not retrievable. I cannot state whether they prescribe a number. NCQA and URAC standards need to be purchased or checked with an accreditation lead.
- No state contract cap specific to RNs was found. All caps seen are role-based.

### Unverified (seen only in snippets or on blocked pages; do not assert)
- Arizona AHCCCS ALTCS-EPD: weighted caseload not to exceed 96; up to 43 members; SMI cap 32 (test.azahcccs.gov RFP, HTTP 403). Arizona behavioral health 10:1 SMI, 25:1 children (AMPM 570-A, HTTP 403).
- AHRQ Medicaid care management guide: 500:1 telephonic-only to 25:1 intensive in-person; Kansas nurse care manager max 60 (ahrq.gov mm5.html, HTTP 403). Highly relevant. **Worth opening manually, as 500:1 would extend the brief's upper bound.**
- Louisiana: care management staff at one per 50,000 enrollees (LDH manual, HTTP 403). This appears to be a local-presence staffing rule, not a caseload.
- TennCare CHOICES: uses weighted caseload with acuity levels; the numeric targets were not seen (tn.gov, HTTP 403).
- MACPAC 2019, care coordinator ratio variability across integrated care programs (the PDF is encrypted and I could not extract it).
- ACL spotlight: CTI 25-30 clients per month; Transitional Care Model 18-20 per nurse; nurse care coordinator 40-50 at a time (PMC6559370 blocked).
- allnurses: telephonic CM 40-80; CLIMB: telephonic up to 500. Low-quality sources, and not recommended even if opened.

## 2. What share of time goes to documentation, data gathering and systems versus patient contact?

### Takeaway
**I found no published time-motion or time-use study of payer RN case managers.** The closest payer-side number is a state rate model, not a measurement: NC assumes equal time for member contact (36%) and for travel, documentation and preparation (36%). Everything measured comes from hospital nurses and should be labeled a proxy.

### Cited Findings

**Closest to payer (assumed, not observed)**
- [PAYER-ADJACENT, model assumption] NC DHHS example time allocation for a care manager with all moderate-acuity BH members: member contact **36%**; travel/documentation/preparation **36%**; care team meetings and additional clinical time **10%**; non-member activities **13%**; non-productive time for caseload turnover **5%**. Other assumptions: **1 hour per telephonic contact (30 min contact + 30 min preparation and documentation)**; 2 hours per in-person contact; 15-45 min per member per month with the care team; 5 hours per week non-member work; 95% capacity. Extenders are assumed to absorb 20% / 30% / 40% of productive time in high / medium / low tiers. January 19, 2022 — [NC DHHS](https://medicaid.ncdhhs.gov/updated-tailored-care-management-rate-guidance-jan-24-2022/download).

**Payer perception data**
- [PAYER] [VENDOR] 3 in 4 health plan leaders say care managers act more as information gatherers than trusted advisors; nearly half name care manager time on administrative tasks as a top post-discharge challenge; 67% cite low member response; 51% cite fragmented data; only 19% say outreach begins with a complete discharge summary, medications and follow-up plans. PointClickCare-commissioned Sage Growth Partners survey of 150+ plan leaders, blog dated September 10, 2026. **Vendor sells the fix; these are leaders' opinions, not time measures** — [PointClickCare](https://pointclickcare.com/blog/our-markets/health-plans/what-care-managers-could-be-doing-instead-of-chasing-charts/).

**Hospital nurses (proxy only)**
- [PROXY-HOSPITAL] 36 med-surg units, 17 systems, 15 states, 767 nurses (763 completing), June 2005-June 2006: documentation **35.3% of nursing practice time** (147.5 min; 27.5% of all reported time); care coordination **20.6%** (86 min); medication administration 17.2% (72 min); patient care activities 19.3% (81 min); assessment and vitals 7.2%. Nurse station 38.6% of time. Hendrich et al., Permanente Journal, 2008 — [PMC3037121](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3037121/).
- [PROXY-HOSPITAL] One Midwest academic med-surg unit, 15 RNs, 316 observed hours: documentation about **25%** of time, including EHR and paper charting and review. Published 2018 — [PMC6371290](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6371290/).
- [PROXY-HOSPITAL] Southeastern US academic system on Epic: EHR time **157 min (2019) and 162 min (2020) per 12-hour shift** from vendor audit logs (the vendor method is not a validated tool). Equals about 22% of a 720-minute shift, my calculation. In the same system, RNs *self-reported* about **43%** of the shift on documentation and said **45% of that documentation was duplicative or unnecessary** (n=1,095, 20.4% response, July 2020). A redesign cut EHR time 18.5% at 3 months — [PMC9300261](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9300261).

### Inferences
- **Suggested estimate for a payer CM's day (8 h), adapted from NC and clearly a modeled figure:** live member contact about 2.9 h; preparation, documentation and (for field staff) travel about 2.9 h; care team and additional clinical work about 0.8 h; meetings and admin about 1.0 h; caseload-turnover slack about 0.4 h. For a purely telephonic CM the 1:1 ratio of contact to preparation and documentation still holds under NC's assumptions. A deck could use "about one hour of preparation and documentation for each hour of member conversation" if it is labeled as a state rate assumption.
- **Telephonic staff likely have a worse contact ratio than this model implies.** Failed outreach is not separately budgeted by NC, and plans report low member response as the top challenge (67%, vendor survey). Reach rates were not found, so this cannot be quantified yet.
- The measured vs perceived gap matters: 22% in logs vs 43% self-reported in the same health system. Survey-based claims of "half the day on paperwork" should be expected to run high, and logs miss off-EHR work. A product pilot should measure with event logs *and* observation.
- The brief's "25-41% of shift" lower bound holds for hospital nurses. Do not transfer it to payers without the proxy label.

### Gaps
- **No time-motion study, audit-log study or diary study of payer case managers was found.** A proprietary time study at a design partner is likely the only way to fill this.
- **CCMC Role & Function time allocation not obtained.** Search snippets describe the study as recurring on a roughly five-year cycle and say results include direct client contact share, but I could not read the figures. Full articles are behind paywalls (HTTP 402 on ceconnection PDFs). The CCMC findings page rendered navigation only. These may report direct client contact share and should be purchased or requested.

### Unverified
- The upper "41%" figure in the brief: no source was found. The closest verified numbers are 35.3% (measured) and 43% (self-reported).
- JMIR Nursing 2025, NYU Langone: flowsheet time 31.11% of a 12-hour shift (page returned empty).
- UPMC observation of 79 nurses: documenting in the EHR 11.5%, technology about a third of time (Becker's, HTTP 403).
- UCHealth: over 30% of a 12-hour shift in the EHR, 18 min saved.
- Baumann et al. 2018 meta-analysis: nurses' documentation share 9% to 23% after EHR implementation (abstract seen in a snippet only).
- CCMC 2014 study sample of 7,723, and 88.8% nursing background (snippets).
- rentry.co and termbin.com pages asserting case managers spend 25-40% on payer documentation: unsourced, apparently machine-generated. **Do not use.**
- Excluded as non-US: Finnish home-care nurse time study (PMC7754451); Australian aged-care case manager survey (PMC4818942).

## 3. How many systems, how long is preparation, and how long are HRAs and assessments?

### Takeaway
The one payer-specific systems study (2012-13) shows multi-system use is the top reported obstacle, with 68% rating duplicate entry and similar inefficiency as a major barrier. No source gives a current count of systems or screens per case manager. Member-facing HRA times run 5-30 minutes; no source reports how long a nurse-led comprehensive assessment takes.

### Cited Findings

**Systems**
- [PAYER] Survey of care managers at a major central Pennsylvania health plan, working in outpatient clinics, hospitals and a Transitions of Care telephonic call center: 80 of 131 responded (61%), December 2012-January 2013, published 2017; authors affiliated with Geisinger — [PMC6557451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6557451/).
  - Systems used: CM software **100%**; EHR **95%**; other claims and authorization systems **35%**; HIE **26%**.
  - Rated a major barrier: inefficient use of multiple systems, e.g. duplicate entry **68%**; not all hospitals and clinics in HIE **60%**; logging into multiple systems **49%**; transferring data between EHR, HIE and CM software **45%**; universal authorization status 42%; timely access to information **37%**; finding correct information for med reconciliation 30%; documenting in CM software 22%.
  - Category scores (0-100): multiple systems 60.3; access inefficiencies 50.9; inefficient CM software 41.4; lack of training 28.8.
  - Important counterpoint: **after controls, obstacles explained only 2-4% of variance in workload, burnout and turnover intention, and were not significant.** Do not claim from this study that fixing systems reduces burnout. HIE use did correlate with lower workload (r=-0.33, p<0.01).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Virginia MCO care coordinators described data integration problems across systems within and between MCOs; no count given. Data 2022 — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/challenges-faced-by-medicaid-managed-care-coordinators-working-with-members-with-substance-use-disorder).

**Preparation per contact or case**
- [PAYER-ADJACENT, assumption] **30 minutes of preparation and documentation per 30-minute telephonic contact**, up to 1 hour of travel, preparation and documentation per 1-hour in-person contact. NC DHHS, 2022 — [NC DHHS](https://medicaid.ncdhhs.gov/updated-tailored-care-management-rate-guidance-jan-24-2022/download).

**HRA length (member-facing)**
- [PAYER] Humana: about **5 minutes**; online, or by phone with a support assistant. Page read November 2026 — [Humana](https://www.humana.com/home-care/primary-preventive-care/telephonic-care-management/health-survey).
- [PAYER] Priority Health D-SNP: **40 questions, about 30 minutes**; digital, paper or phone. Page read November 2026 — [Priority Health](https://www.priorityhealth.com/medicare/once-you-enroll/prioritymedicare-dsnp/plan-features/hra).

**Assessment timing rules and contact frequency**
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Ohio Medicaid: HRA within **90 calendar days** of effective enrollment, then at least annually. Rev. 7/2026 — [Ohio Medicaid](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/medicaid.ohio.gov/Providers/ProviderTypes/Managed%20Care/Provider%20Agreements/2026_07_MCO_Final_2.pdf).
- [PAYER] Blue Shield Promise Medi-Cal complex CM: assessment **initiated within 30 calendar days** of eligibility identification, **completed within 60**; contact at least **every 30 days** for engaged members, typically **every 14 days**, every 3 months for monitored non-engaged members. Revised 4/2026 — [Blue Shield Promise](https://www.blueshieldca.com/content/dam/bsca/en/promise/clinical-policies-procedures/PHMCM-002-Complex-case-management.pdf).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] NC contact minimums per month: BH high at least 4, medium at least 3, low at least 2; I/DD or TBI high at least 3, medium at least 3, low at least 1 — [NC DHHS](https://medicaid.ncdhhs.gov/updated-tailored-care-management-rate-guidance-jan-24-2022/download).

### Inferences
- Summing system-use shares from the Pennsylvania survey gives a mean of about **2.6 named systems per care manager**, my calculation. That was in 2012-13 at one integrated plan with a shared EHR, and it excludes phone, email, portals and spreadsheets, so **treat it as a floor**. A current count would need field work.
- A 5-minute and a 30-minute HRA are different instruments, so "HRA length" should not be quoted as a single number. The nurse's time per HRA also includes outreach, review and care plan follow-up, none of which those pages measure.

### Gaps
- **No published count of systems or screens per case manager, and no KLAS report on this topic, was found.** Search returned nothing from KLAS.
- **No source gives time-to-complete for a nurse-administered comprehensive assessment, or preparation time before a first assessment.** The commonly quoted "60 minutes" appears to come from the CPT 99487 billing threshold, not assessment duration, and I did not open a source confirming this.

### Unverified
- CMS MLN909188 and AAFP on CCM: 60-minute complex and 20-minute non-complex thresholds (snippets; billing rules rather than workload evidence).
- AAFP 2012: Medicare HRA tool under 10 minutes (snippet).
- SNP Model of Care requiring HRA within 90 days of enrollment, annual repeat (plan pages seen as snippets only).

## 4. Turnover, cost of turnover, wages and burnout

### Takeaway
All turnover and cost figures are for hospital RNs; **no payer case manager turnover rate was found**. Latest bedside figures are 17.6% RN turnover and $60,090 per departure. Insurer-employed RNs earned a $90,070 mean in May 2023 (latest BLS table I could read at industry level), and national case manager estimates run $77k-85k. Burnout evidence suggests non-hospital settings fare better, with administrative burden as the strongest correlate in the one recent Medicaid MCO study.

### Cited Findings

**Turnover and cost**
- [PROXY-HOSPITAL] NSI 2026 report (527 hospitals, 40 states, 262,405 RNs; calendar 2025 data; acute care hospitals only): RN turnover **17.6%**, up 1.2 points; hospital all-staff turnover 18.5%; first-year RN turnover 22.7%; **cost per bedside RN departure $60,090**; average annual hospital cost **$5.19M** (range $4.2M-$6.2M); **$294,976** per 1-point change; RN vacancy **8.6%**; **time to recruit an experienced RN 56-102 days** by specialty. RN specialties charted do not include case management. The report is inconsistent on full/part-time RN turnover (14.5% in text, 14.6% in table). NSI is a recruiter — [NSI 2026](https://www.nsinursingsolutions.com/documents/library/nsi_national_health_care_retention_report.pdf).
- [PROXY-HOSPITAL] Press Ganey State of Nursing 2026: RN turnover about **17%**; about 1 in 5 early-career nurses leaving; 500,000+ nurses and APPs; May 12, 2026. No burnout percentage on this page — [Press Ganey](https://www.pressganey.com/resources/news/nursing-engagement-improves-while-early-career-nurse-departures-persist-at-alarming-rates/).
- [PAYER] Weak signal only: care managers at the Pennsylvania plan averaged 2.75 years of tenure and 2.79 years in case management; 46.2 mean hours per week (range 35-80). 2012-13 — [PMC6557451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6557451/).

**Burnout and working conditions**
- [PAYER] Pennsylvania plan: mean workload 59.4, burnout 48.2, turnover intention 26.7 (2012-13; scale details in the paper). Inefficient CM software correlated with workload at r=0.24 (p<0.05) — [PMC6557451](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6557451/).
- [PAYER-ADJACENT] Virginia MCO care coordinators reported **high job satisfaction and low burnout overall**, yet **administrative and regulatory burden was the barrier most strongly tied to burnout (r=-0.55 as reported)** and was higher where caseloads grew substantially. 2022 — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/challenges-faced-by-medicaid-managed-care-coordinators-working-with-members-with-substance-use-disorder).
- [PROXY-OTHER, all RNs] 2018 NSSRN (3,957,661 weighted RNs): **31.5%** of nurses who left a job in 2017 (n=418,769) cited burnout. Adjusted odds of leaving due to burnout, compared with clinic settings: hospital 2.10 (1.41-3.13), other inpatient 2.26 (1.39-3.68), **"other settings" (which includes insurers) 1.33 (0.82-2.16, not significant)**. Shah et al., JAMA Netw Open 2021 — [PMC7862989](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7862989/).
- [PROXY-OTHER, all RNs] NCSBN 2024 study (~800,000 nurses, 53 jurisdictions): 39.9% of RNs intend to leave or retire within five years; about 41.5% of those leaving (excluding retirement) cite stress and burnout. ### Inferences
- **Loaded hourly cost for sizing (my calculation):** BLS insurer mean $43.30/h x 1.29 benefits load (from NC's 29%) is about **$56/h**. Payscale $84,959 / 2,080 h x 1.29 is about **$53/h**. Use **$53-56/h** with the May 2023 and self-report caveats; add NC's 15% overhead if a fully loaded rate is needed. So at about 2.9 h/day of preparation and documentation, a CM costs about $150-160/day, or about $37k-$40k/year over 240 days, in that category. This is an upper-bound addressable pool, not expected savings, and includes travel for field staff.
- Insurer RN pay ran about 5% below the all-RN mean in May 2023 (90,070 vs 94,480). Payscale's CM average sits about 13% below the May 2025 RN median, but the two datasets differ in method, so only the direction is usable.
- Do **not** present $60,090 as payer replacement cost. It reflects hospital backfill, including premium labor. Payer roles are generally non-shift remote or office work, so the true figure could differ in either direction. Payer turnover should be asked directly from a design partner.
- A "burned-out payer nurse" narrative is not well supported. Better supported: administrative burden is the main burnout *correlate*, and caseloads are growing. The stronger pitch is capacity and member reach, with burnout secondary.
